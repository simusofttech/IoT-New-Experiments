Aim

To develop a Wi-Fi-enabled water-level monitoring and alert system using an ESP32 development board, analog water level sensor, SSD1306 OLED display and active buzzer. The system measures relative water level, classifies it into LOW, MEDIUM and HIGH conditions, and displays live information through a web dashboard.

About the Experiment

The Wi-Fi Water-Level Alert experiment demonstrates analog sensor interfacing, ADC conversion, water-level classification, audible alarms and wireless data monitoring using an ESP32 microcontroller.

An analog water level sensor connected to GPIO34 produces a variable voltage depending on water contact with its sensing tracks.

The ESP32 reads this analog voltage using its built-in 12-bit ADC and converts the raw reading into an approximate water-level percentage.

The calculated value is classified into three states: LOW, MEDIUM and HIGH.

A 128×64 SSD1306 OLED connected through I²C displays the relative percentage and current water-level classification.

When the calculated percentage reaches 70% or more, an active buzzer connected to GPIO25 is activated.

The ESP32 connects to Wi-Fi and hosts a browser dashboard that displays the percentage, classification, raw ADC reading and IP address.

Important:

The displayed percentage is a relative ADC-based measurement, not a calibrated water depth, tank capacity or physical water-height measurement.

The original sketch maps ADC values from 0–4095 directly to 0–100%. Actual sensors may require calibration to produce meaningful threshold values.

Hardware Precautions:

GPIO34 is an input-only ADC1 pin and is suitable for analog sensing while the ESP32 Wi-Fi is active.

Power the water sensor from its compatible supply and ensure that its analog output never exceeds 3.3V at GPIO34.

Use an active buzzer that can be safely driven by a 3.3V GPIO, or add an appropriate transistor driver for a higher-current buzzer.

Avoid prolonged exposure of basic resistive water sensors to water because the electrodes may corrode over time.

Water Level Measurement

The analog water sensor generates a variable voltage based on water contact with its sensing area.

Analog-to-Digital Conversion

The ESP32 uses the ADC on GPIO34 to convert the sensor signal into a digital reading.

Percentage Calculation

The raw ADC reading is mapped into an approximate relative percentage from 0 to 100%.

Water-Level Classification

Readings below 30% are classified as LOW, readings from 30% to below 70% are MEDIUM, and readings at 70% or above are HIGH.

Buzzer Alert

When the level enters the HIGH range, GPIO25 activates the active buzzer.

OLED and Dashboard

The OLED shows the percentage and status locally. The web dashboard displays the same monitoring values over the local Wi-Fi network.

Procedure

  1. Arrange the ESP32 development board, analog water-level sensor, SSD1306 OLED, active buzzer, breadboard and jumper wires.
  2. Connect the sensor VCC to 3V3.
  3. Connect the sensor GND to ESP32 GND.
  4. Connect the sensor SIG/AO to GPIO34.
  5. Connect OLED VCC to 3V3.
  6. Connect OLED GND to GND.
  7. Connect OLED SDA to GPIO21.
  8. Connect OLED SCL to GPIO22.
  9. Connect the buzzer signal to GPIO25, using a driver if required.
  10. Connect buzzer GND to common ground.
  11. Verify the sensor output voltage and circuit connections.
  12. Open Arduino IDE.
  13. Install the ESP32 board support package.
  14. Install the U8g2 library.
  15. Select the appropriate ESP32 development board.
  16. Copy the complete sketch from Explore the Code.
  17. Enter your Wi-Fi SSID and password.
  18. Verify and upload the firmware.
  19. Open Serial Monitor at 115200 baud.
  20. Note the ESP32 IP address after Wi-Fi connection.
  21. Open the IP address in a browser connected to the same network.
  22. Observe the water-level percentage on the OLED and dashboard.
  23. Gradually immerse the sensing section of the water sensor.
  24. Observe LOW, MEDIUM and HIGH classification changes.
  25. Verify that the buzzer activates when the calculated level reaches 70%.
  26. Remove the sensor from the water and observe the updated readings.

Result

The Wi-Fi Water-Level Alert experiment demonstrates relative water-level monitoring using an analog sensor and ESP32 development board.

The system classifies the calculated sensor percentage into LOW, MEDIUM and HIGH states. The SSD1306 OLED displays local measurements, while the web dashboard provides wireless monitoring of sensor values.

When the calculated percentage reaches 70% or more, the active buzzer produces a high-water-level warning.
