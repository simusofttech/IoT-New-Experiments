Aim

To develop a light-activated camera experiment using an AI-Thinker ESP32-CAM, an LDR, a 10KΩ resistor and an SSD1306 OLED. The system monitors ambient light and automatically captures a JPEG image when the light level changes by at least 15 percentage points.

About the Experiment

The Light-Activated Camera experiment uses a Light Dependent Resistor to detect changes in ambient light.

The LDR and 10KΩ resistor form a voltage divider connected to GPIO2 of the ESP32-CAM.

The ESP32-CAM reads the analog voltage and converts its 12-bit ADC value into an approximate light percentage from 0 to 100%.

The current light value is compared with a stored reference reading. When the absolute difference is 15 percentage points or more, the onboard camera captures a JPEG frame.

The SSD1306 OLED continuously displays the approximate light percentage, monitoring status and number of successful captures.

After a successful threshold event, the reference light value is updated and monitoring continues.

Important:

This firmware does not enable Wi-Fi because GPIO2 is an ADC2 input on the original ESP32. ADC2 measurements can conflict with Wi-Fi operation.

The captured JPEG is held briefly in the camera frame buffer and then returned to the camera driver. No image file is saved permanently or made available through a browser.

The light percentage is a relative ADC-based estimate, not a calibrated lux measurement.

Hardware Precautions:

The LDR and resistor must form a voltage divider between 3.3V and GND. Do not apply 5V directly to GPIO2.

GPIO2 is also a boot-strapping pin. The LDR voltage divider may hold GPIO2 HIGH under bright light, interfering with firmware download mode on some ESP32-CAM setups. Check boot behavior and, when needed, isolate the divider during programming.

The programmer must use 3.3V UART signal levels. If powering the ESP32-CAM from the programmer's 5V output, verify its current capability.

Light Sensing

An LDR changes resistance depending on the amount of light falling on it. The voltage divider converts this resistance change into a variable analog voltage.

Analog Conversion

GPIO2 reads the voltage using the ESP32 ADC. The reading is mapped to an approximate 0–100% light value.

Threshold Comparison

The program calculates the absolute difference between the current light percentage and the stored reference value.

A difference of at least 15 percentage points triggers an image capture.

Camera Operation

The ESP32-CAM camera is initialized in JPEG mode. A frame is captured when the light-change threshold is reached.

OLED Status

The OLED shows the current light percentage and total successful capture count. During a capture, it displays CAPTURE! and the count.

Image Handling

The camera buffer is returned immediately after the capture information is printed. No permanent image archive or browser-viewable image is created by this firmware.

Procedure

  1. Arrange the ESP32-CAM, LDR, 10KΩ resistor, SSD1306 OLED, programmer, breadboard and jumper wires.
  2. Connect one leg of the LDR to 3.3V.
  3. Connect the other LDR leg to GPIO2.
  4. Connect one leg of the 10KΩ resistor to GPIO2.
  5. Connect the other resistor leg to GND.
  6. Connect OLED VCC to 3.3V.
  7. Connect OLED GND to common ground.
  8. Connect OLED SDA to GPIO13.
  9. Connect OLED SCL to GPIO14.
  10. Connect the USB-to-TTL programmer to the ESP32-CAM UART pins.
  11. Place the ESP32-CAM in flashing mode by connecting GPIO0 to GND and restarting the board.
  12. Verify that the GPIO2 divider does not prevent flashing mode.
  13. Open Arduino IDE.
  14. Install the ESP32 board package.
  15. Install Adafruit GFX and Adafruit SSD1306 libraries.
  16. Select AI Thinker ESP32-CAM as the board.
  17. Create the sketch ESP32_CAM_Light_Activated_Camera.ino.
  18. Copy the complete program from Explore the Code.
  19. Verify and upload the program.
  20. Disconnect GPIO0 from GND and restart the board.
  21. Open Serial Monitor at 115200 baud.
  22. Observe the startup display on the OLED.
  23. Allow the system to record its initial light reference.
  24. Change the ambient light around the LDR.
  25. Observe the light percentage in Serial Monitor and on the OLED.
  26. Change light enough to cross the 15-point threshold.
  27. Observe the JPEG capture message and increased counter.
  28. Repeat with another significant change in light.

Explore More: LDR

An LDR changes its resistance with incident light. A voltage divider converts that resistance change into an ADC input.

Explore More: ADC

The ESP32 ADC converts an analog voltage into a numerical value. The program maps its reading to an approximate percentage.

Explore More: Threshold

A threshold of 15 percentage points means a change from 40% to 55% or from 40% to 25% can trigger capture.

Explore More: Image Storage

The camera frame buffer contains the captured JPEG. This sketch returns the buffer after reading its size and does not save the photograph.

Result

The Light-Activated Camera experiment demonstrates automatic image capture based on changes in ambient light measured through an LDR voltage divider.

The ESP32-CAM checks the light level at one-second intervals and captures a JPEG image when the difference from the reference reaches 15 percentage points.

The SSD1306 OLED displays the approximate light percentage and successful capture count. The sketch does not permanently store the images.
