Aim

To build a Light Following LED Indicator using Arduino UNO, two LDR sensors and two LEDs.

The experiment compares light intensity from the left and right sides and switches ON the LED corresponding to the side receiving more light.

About the Experiment

An LDR, or Light Dependent Resistor, changes its resistance according to the amount of light falling on its surface.

When light intensity increases, the resistance of a typical LDR decreases.

This experiment uses two LDRs, one for the left side and one for the right side.

Each LDR forms a voltage divider with a 10kΩ resistor.

Arduino measures the voltage of the left divider through A1 and the right divider through A0.

The readings are compared using conditional statements.

If the left reading is higher, the left LED turns ON.

If the right reading is higher, the right LED turns ON.

When the two digital readings are exactly equal, both LEDs remain OFF.

The readings are also printed to the Serial Monitor at 9600 baud.

The experiment introduces analog sensing, voltage dividers, comparison operators and digital output control.

Important:

With the specified wiring, each LDR connects from 5V to its analog input, while the 10kΩ resistor connects from that input to GND.

This configuration produces a higher analog voltage when the corresponding LDR receives more light.

The code uses exact equality for the balanced condition. Small sensor variations can cause either LED to activate even when both sides appear similarly bright.

Voltage Divider Wiring:

Each LDR must form a complete voltage divider with its 10kΩ resistor.

The analog input connects to the junction between the LDR and resistor.

Do not connect the analog input to a floating LDR terminal without the pull-down resistor.

Both LED cathodes must connect to GND, and each LED requires its own 220Ω current-limiting resistor.

LDR Operation

Each LDR responds to light by changing its resistance.

When more light falls on an LDR, its resistance typically decreases.

The LDR and 10kΩ resistor produce a voltage that changes with light intensity.

Analog Readings

Arduino uses analogRead() to measure each voltage divider.

The Arduino UNO analog-to-digital converter produces readings from 0 to 1023.

The leftLight variable stores the reading from A1.

The rightLight variable stores the reading from A0.

Comparing Light Levels

Arduino compares the two readings using greater-than conditions.

When leftLight is greater than rightLight, D7 becomes HIGH and D8 becomes LOW.

When rightLight is greater than leftLight, D8 becomes HIGH and D7 becomes LOW.

When both values are exactly equal, both outputs become LOW.

Voltage Divider Formula

Approximate Divider Relationship:

Vout = 5V × (10kΩ / (LDR Resistance + 10kΩ))

With the LDR connected to 5V and the 10kΩ resistor to GND, a lower LDR resistance produces a higher output voltage.

Serial Monitor

The program prints both analog values at 9600 baud.

Students can observe how the sensor readings change when a torch or other light source is moved between the two LDRs.

Important Program Behavior

The original program compares raw sensor values directly.

It does not use a difference threshold or sensor calibration.

As a result, small differences caused by component tolerances or electrical noise may switch the indicated side.

Procedure

  1. Arrange Arduino UNO R3, two LDRs, two LEDs, two 10kΩ resistors, two 220Ω resistors, a breadboard and jumper wires.
  2. Place the two LDR sensors on the breadboard.
  3. Position one LDR to represent the left direction.
  4. Position the other LDR to represent the right direction.
  5. Connect one terminal of the left LDR to Arduino 5V.
  6. Connect its other terminal to A1.
  7. Connect a 10kΩ resistor between A1 and GND.
  8. Connect one terminal of the right LDR to Arduino 5V.
  9. Connect its other terminal to A0.
  10. Connect another 10kΩ resistor between A0 and GND.
  11. Connect the left LED anode to D7 through a 220Ω resistor.
  12. Connect the left LED cathode to GND.
  13. Connect the right LED anode to D8 through a separate 220Ω resistor.
  14. Connect the right LED cathode to GND.
  15. Verify the complete circuit wiring.
  16. Open Arduino IDE.
  17. Create a sketch named Light_Following_LED_Indicator.ino.
  18. Copy the original Arduino program from Explore the Code.
  19. Select Arduino Uno and the correct communication port.
  20. Verify and upload the program.
  21. Open Serial Monitor at 9600 baud.
  22. Shine more light on the left LDR.
  23. Observe the left LED turning ON.
  24. Shine more light on the right LDR.
  25. Observe the right LED turning ON.
  26. Try to provide similar illumination to both LDRs.
  27. Observe the changes in sensor readings and LED selection.
  28. Record the results and compare the analog values.

Result

The Light Following LED Indicator experiment demonstrates comparison of light intensity from two directions.

Arduino UNO reads two LDR-based voltage dividers and switches ON the LED corresponding to the sensor producing the higher analog value.

When both readings are exactly equal, both LEDs remain OFF.

The experiment illustrates light sensing, analog input, conditional decisions and LED indication.
