Aim

To design and develop an Ultrasonic Water Tank Level Indicator using Arduino UNO, an HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor and four LEDs.

The experiment measures the distance between the ultrasonic sensor and the water surface, calculates the estimated water level and percentage, and displays different tank-level conditions using LED indicators.

About the Experiment

The Ultrasonic Water Tank Level Indicator demonstrates how Arduino UNO can estimate the water level in a tank without directly placing the measurement sensor inside the water.

An HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor is installed above the water surface, facing downward.

It emits ultrasonic pulses and receives echoes reflected by the water surface.

Arduino measures the echo duration and calculates the distance between the sensor and the water surface.

The original program uses a tank height of 100 cm.

The estimated water level is calculated by subtracting the measured distance from the tank height.

The water level is then converted into a percentage.

Four LEDs indicate increasing water level categories.

When the tank percentage is below 25%, all LEDs remain OFF.

At 25%, LED 1 turns ON.

At 50%, LED 1 and LED 2 remain ON.

At 75%, the first three LEDs turn ON.

At 95% or above, all four LEDs turn ON.

Arduino also prints the measured distance, estimated water level and tank percentage to Serial Monitor at 9600 baud.

Important:

The tankHeight variable is set to 100.0 cm in the original Arduino program.

This value assumes that the sensor's measurement origin is approximately 100 cm above the reference tank bottom.

The result represents a height-based percentage, not a direct measurement of water volume.

For tanks with irregular shapes, the height percentage may not equal the volume percentage.

The HC-SR04 should be protected from splashing, condensation and direct water exposure.

Ultrasonic Sensor Operation

The HC-SR04 sends a short ultrasonic sound pulse toward the water surface.

The reflected ultrasonic pulse returns to the sensor receiver.

Arduino measures the ECHO signal duration using pulseIn().

Distance Calculation

The original sketch calculates distance using the following formula:

Distance Formula:

Distance (cm) = Echo Duration × 0.034 / 2

0.034 represents the approximate sound speed in centimeters per microsecond.

Dividing by 2 accounts for the outward and return paths of the ultrasonic pulse.

Water Level Calculation

The water level is determined by subtracting the distance from the configured tank height.

Water Level Formula:

Water Level = Tank Height − Distance

The original sketch sets Tank Height = 100 cm.

For example, if the measured distance is 30 cm:

Water Level = 100 − 30 = 70 cm.

Preventing Values Outside the Tank Height

The sketch limits the calculated waterLevel variable to values between zero and tankHeight.

If a calculated water level is negative, it is replaced with zero.

If it exceeds tankHeight, it is replaced with tankHeight.

Water Level Percentage

Arduino converts the water level into a percentage by dividing it by the tank height.

Percentage Formula:

Tank Level (%) = (Water Level / Tank Height) × 100

If the water level is 70 cm and the tank height is 100 cm:

Tank Level = 70%.

Serial Monitoring

Arduino prints the sensor distance, estimated water level and percentage during each measurement cycle.

The serial communication baud rate is 9600.

Measurement Interval

The original program uses delay(500) after each measurement and LED update.

The actual interval also includes the ultrasonic echo measurement time.

Source-Code Limitation:

The program calls pulseIn(echoPin, HIGH) without an explicit timeout argument or a check for missing echoes.

pulseIn() returns zero when no pulse is detected. The source then calculates distance as zero, resulting in a reported water level of 100 cm and 100% full.

This is a limitation of the original sketch, which is preserved unchanged. The readings should not be used for safety-critical tank overflow protection.

Procedure

  1. Arrange Arduino UNO, HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor, four LEDs, four 220Ω resistors, breadboard and jumper wires.
  2. Connect the HC-SR04 VCC terminal to Arduino 5V.
  3. Connect the HC-SR04 GND terminal to Arduino GND.
  4. Connect the TRIG terminal to digital pin D9.
  5. Connect the ECHO terminal to digital pin D10.
  6. Connect LED 1 anode to D2 through a 220Ω resistor.
  7. Connect LED 2 anode to D3 through a separate 220Ω resistor.
  8. Connect LED 3 anode to D4 through a 220Ω resistor.
  9. Connect LED 4 anode to D5 through a separate 220Ω resistor.
  10. Connect all four LED cathodes to ground.
  11. Verify the sensor wiring and LED polarity before powering the circuit.
  12. Position the sensor above the tank, facing downward toward the water surface.
  13. Measure the usable vertical distance between the sensor reference point and tank bottom.
  14. The original program assumes this distance is 100 cm.
  15. Open Arduino IDE.
  16. Create a sketch named Ultrasonic_Water_Tank_Level_Indicator.ino.
  17. Copy the complete Arduino program from Explore the Code.
  18. Select Arduino Uno and the correct communication port.
  19. Verify and upload the sketch.
  20. Open Serial Monitor and select 9600 baud.
  21. Observe the measured distance, calculated water level and percentage values.
  22. Use a safe dry simulation with a flat object, or vary the water level under controlled conditions.
  23. Observe how the LEDs activate progressively as the calculated level increases.
  24. Check that all LEDs are OFF below 25%.
  25. Verify that LED 1 turns ON at 25%.
  26. Verify that LED 2 also turns ON at 50%.
  27. Verify that LED 3 turns ON at 75%.
  28. Verify that LED 4 turns ON at 95%.
  29. Compare the Serial Monitor values with the LED status table.
  30. Check how the system behaves if the ultrasonic echo is lost.
  31. Record the limitations of the unchanged source code.

Explore more: Water Level Formula

The sensor measures the empty space between the top-mounted sensor and the water surface.

Water Level = Tank Height − Measured Distance.

The formula assumes the sensor is mounted at the intended tank reference height.

Explore more: Percentage Calculation

The height-based percentage is calculated from the ratio of water level to tank height.

Percentage = Water Level / Tank Height × 100.

The source uses 100 cm for its tank height.

Explore more: LED Thresholds

The sketch activates LEDs in stages.

One LED turns ON at 25%, two at 50%, three at 75%, and all four at 95%.

All LEDs remain OFF below 25%.

Explore more: Missing Echo

The original program does not explicitly check whether pulseIn() receives a valid echo.

A zero return can be calculated as zero distance, which the formula interprets as 100% full.

This should be addressed before using the system for real overflow detection.

No-Echo Behavior in the Original Sketch

When pulseIn() returns zero, the source calculates the following values: 

Distance: 0.00 cm | Water Level: 100.00 cm | Tank Level: 100.00%

All four LEDs would turn ON despite the lack of a valid distance measurement.

Important Output Notes:

The example readings assume a 100 cm tank height.

Real HC-SR04 readings may vary due to surface reflections, sensor position and environmental conditions.

At very short distances, the HC-SR04 may not provide reliable measurements.

The program has no dedicated no-echo error indicator.

The four LEDs represent discrete percentage categories, not a continuously varying light bar.

Result

The Ultrasonic Water Tank Level Indicator experiment demonstrates measurement of the distance between an HC-SR04 sensor and a water surface using Arduino UNO.

The program calculates the estimated water level by subtracting the measured distance from a predefined tank height of 100 cm.

The estimated height is converted into a percentage and compared with programmed thresholds.

Four LEDs indicate the corresponding percentage ranges, while the Serial Monitor displays the measured distance, water level and tank percentage.

The experiment demonstrates ultrasonic distance measurement, mathematical conversion, percentage calculation, conditional logic and multiple LED output control.

Before real tank monitoring, the original sketch's missing-echo handling should be improved.
