Aim

To build an automatic electronic proximity doorbell using Arduino UNO, an HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor, a servo motor and a buzzer.

The system activates when a person or object is detected at a distance of 40 cm or less.

About the Experiment

The Electronic Proximity Doorbell demonstrates automatic activation of a doorbell based on ultrasonic distance measurements.

The HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor sends a sound pulse and measures the time required for its echo to return.

Arduino converts the echo duration into an estimated distance in centimeters.

When a person or object comes within the programmed detection distance of 40 cm, the doorbell sequence begins.

The buzzer generates two successive tones, followed by two movements of a servo motor representing mechanical bell strikes.

Once the doorbell has activated, a detection-state variable prevents it from repeatedly triggering while the object remains within the threshold.

The system becomes ready for another activation when the measured distance exceeds 40 cm.

Important Hardware Note: The sketch uses tone() to generate signals at 1000 Hz and 1500 Hz. A passive buzzer can reproduce these tones, whereas a typical active buzzer has a built-in oscillator and may not reproduce distinct pitches. Confirm the buzzer type used in your actual experiment.

Distance Measurement

Arduino sends a 10-microsecond trigger pulse to the ultrasonic sensor through D9.

The HC-SR04 sends an ultrasonic signal and produces an ECHO pulse corresponding to the round-trip travel time of the sound.

Arduino reads this duration through D10.

Distance Formula:

Distance (cm) = Echo Duration × 0.0343 / 2

The division by 2 accounts for the sound traveling to the object and back.

Doorbell Sound

The program requests a 1000 Hz tone for 250 ms, followed by a 1500 Hz tone for 350 ms.

Servo Operation

The servo moves between the programmed rest angle of 30° and strike angle of 110° two times.

Detection State

The variable personDetected prevents repeated activation while an object remains within the detection threshold.

After a reading greater than 40 cm, the program resets the state and allows another doorbell activation.

Sensor Limitation: The original pulseIn() call has no explicit timeout or invalid-reading check. A timeout returns zero, which the sketch converts to 0 cm. This can incorrectly trigger the doorbell when no echo is received.

Procedure

  1. Place Arduino UNO, the ultrasonic sensor, servo, buzzer and breadboard on a stable working surface.
  2. Connect ultrasonic VCC to Arduino 5V.
  3. Connect ultrasonic GND to Arduino GND.
  4. Connect ultrasonic TRIG to digital pin D9.
  5. Connect ultrasonic ECHO to digital pin D10.
  6. Connect servo signal to digital pin D6.
  7. Connect servo VCC to a suitable regulated 5V supply.
  8. Connect servo GND to the common ground.
  9. Verify the buzzer type and operating requirements.
  10. Connect the buzzer positive terminal to D8 if suitable for direct pin drive.
  11. Connect the buzzer negative terminal to GND.
  12. Check all electrical connections and polarity.
  13. Open Arduino IDE.
  14. Create a sketch named Electronic_Proximity_Doorbell.ino.
  15. Copy the complete Arduino sketch from Explore the Code.
  16. Select Arduino Uno and the correct port.
  17. Verify and upload the program.
  18. Open Serial Monitor at 9600 baud.
  19. Place a flat object beyond the 40 cm threshold.
  20. Move the object within 40 cm of the ultrasonic sensor.
  21. Observe the buzzer sequence and servo striking movements.
  22. Move the object beyond 40 cm again.
  23. Verify that the system becomes ready for another detection.

Expected Output

The ultrasonic sensor continuously measures the distance to an object.

When the measured distance is 40 cm or less, the doorbell activates once for that detection event.

The buzzer produces two programmed tone signals. The servo then performs two bell-striking movements.

The system waits until a distance greater than 40 cm is measured before allowing another activation.

Result

The Electronic Proximity Doorbell experiment demonstrates automatic activation of a buzzer and servo motor using ultrasonic distance detection and Arduino UNO.

The program activates the doorbell when an object is measured within 40 cm and becomes ready for another detection after the object moves beyond the threshold.
