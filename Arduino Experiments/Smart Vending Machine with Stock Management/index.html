Aim

To design and simulate a Smart Vending Machine with Stock Management using Arduino UNO, a 16×2 I²C LCD, five push buttons, a servo motor and two stock-indicator LEDs.

The system allows a user to select one of two products, add simulated credit in ₹5 increments, verify sufficient balance and product stock, and initiate a servo dispensing cycle after a valid purchase.

About the Experiment

The Smart Vending Machine with Stock Management is an Arduino-based educational experiment that demonstrates product selection, credit management, purchase validation, stock tracking and actuator control.

The vending machine contains two products: Product A and Product B.

Product A has an initial stock of five items and costs ₹10.

Product B has an initial stock of three items and costs ₹15.

Five push buttons provide the user interface: select A, select B, add credit, cancel a transaction and purchase the selected product.

Each credit-button press increases the simulated balance by ₹5.

The LCD shows the selected product, its price, the remaining stock and the user's available credit.

When BUY is pressed, Arduino verifies whether the selected product is available and the user has enough credit.

If payment is insufficient, the LCD shows how much additional credit is required.

If the product is out of stock, the LCD shows an unavailable product message.

After a valid purchase, Arduino moves the servo from 0° to 90° and back to 0°.

The selected product's simulated stock decreases by one, and its price is deducted from the balance.

Two LEDs indicate when the remaining stock for either product reaches two items or fewer.

Important:

This experiment simulates payment by counting button presses. It does not accept coins, banknotes or digital payments.

Stock values are stored in Arduino RAM and return to their initial values when the controller restarts.

The program does not use an item-detection sensor to confirm physical dispensing.

Button Input Logic:

All five buttons use INPUT_PULLUP.

Arduino normally reads HIGH until the corresponding button connects the input to GND.

A valid press is recognized after a 35 ms stable LOW interval.

Product Selection

The user chooses Product A by pressing the button on D6.

Product B is selected using the button on D7.

The LCD then shows the selected product's price, remaining stock and available balance.

Simulated Credit

Pressing the Credit button on D8 increases the balance variable by 5.

For example, two presses provide ₹10 credit, while three presses provide ₹15 credit.

Credit Formula:

New Balance = Previous Balance + ₹5

This is a simulated credit value stored in Arduino memory.

Purchase Verification

When BUY is pressed, Arduino first checks whether the selected product has stock greater than zero.

If the stock is zero, the LCD displays OUT OF STOCK.

If the product is available but the balance is lower than its price, the LCD displays the additional credit required.

If both conditions are satisfied, Arduino accepts the simulated purchase.

Servo Dispensing Sequence

The servo is initially commanded to 0°, representing the closed position.

During a successful purchase, the servo moves to 90° for one second.

It then returns to 0° and waits another 300 ms.

This demonstrates a single shared dispensing movement for both products.

Stock Management

After dispensing, the stock value for the selected product is decreased by one.

Product A and Product B have independent stock variables.

The LCD displays the updated stock when the status screen returns.

Low-Stock LED Indication

Each product has a dedicated indicator LED.

Arduino switches an indicator ON when its product has two items or fewer remaining.

₹10 Product A price
₹15 Product B price
₹5 Credit per press
≤ 2 Low-stock threshold

Cancel Transaction

Pressing Cancel sets the balance to zero.

The LCD displays Transaction Cancelled before returning to the status screen.

Cancel does not restock products or refund a previous purchase in the simulation.

Program Behavior:

Successful purchases keep unused credit as the remaining balance.

The code contains blocking delays while displaying messages and moving the servo.

No external inventory sensor verifies product movement or physical stock.

Procedure

  1. Arrange Arduino UNO, the 16×2 I²C LCD, servo motor, five push buttons, two LEDs, two 220Ω resistors, breadboard and jumper wires.
  2. Connect the LCD VCC to 5V and GND to GND.
  3. Connect LCD SDA to A4 and SCL to A5.
  4. Connect the Product A selection button between D6 and GND.
  5. Connect the Product B selection button between D7 and GND.
  6. Connect the Credit button between D8 and GND.
  7. Connect the Cancel button between D10 and GND.
  8. Connect the BUY button between D11 and GND.
  9. Connect Product A LED anode to A0 through a 220Ω resistor.
  10. Connect Product B LED anode to A1 through a 220Ω resistor.
  11. Connect both LED cathodes to GND.
  12. Connect the servo signal wire to A3.
  13. Provide a suitable regulated supply for the servo and ensure common ground with Arduino.
  14. Verify that the servo can move freely without obstruction.
  15. Open Arduino IDE.
  16. Install compatible LiquidCrystal_I2C and Servo libraries if required.
  17. Create a sketch named Smart_Vending_Machine_with_Stock_Management.ino.
  18. Copy the complete Arduino program from Explore the Code.
  19. Select Arduino Uno and the correct communication port.
  20. Verify and upload the sketch.
  21. Open Serial Monitor at 9600 baud.
  22. Observe the startup messages on the LCD.
  23. Select Product A and verify the displayed price and stock.
  24. Press Credit twice to add ₹10.
  25. Press BUY and observe the servo cycle.
  26. Verify that Product A stock decreases from 5 to 4.
  27. Select Product B and add sufficient simulated credit.
  28. Press BUY and verify the corresponding stock update.
  29. Test a purchase with insufficient credit and observe the LCD message.
  30. Continue purchasing a product until its stock reaches two items and check its LED.
  31. Continue until stock reaches zero and observe the OUT OF STOCK message.
  32. Press Cancel to clear the remaining simulated balance.
  33. Restart Arduino and observe that the initial stock values return to 5 and 3.

Explore more: INPUT_PULLUP

All five push buttons use Arduino's internal pull-up resistors.

A released button reads HIGH, while a pressed button reads LOW.

Explore more: Button Debouncing

Mechanical buttons may produce several rapid electrical transitions during a single press.

The original sketch waits until a new state has remained stable for 35 ms before accepting it.

It recognizes the transition to LOW as one button press.

Explore more: Stock Variables

stockA and stockB store the available quantity of each product.

Successful purchases reduce the corresponding variable by one.

These values are not saved to nonvolatile memory.

Explore more: Servo Dispensing

The sketch uses the Servo library to control the gate.

gate.write(90) commands the open position.

gate.write(0) commands the closed position.

The program does not confirm actual item movement.

Explore more: Purchase Conditions

Before a purchase, Arduino checks that stock is greater than zero and the balance is at least the product price.

When either condition fails, the program displays a corresponding message instead of moving the servo.

Output Notes:

The system uses simulated payment and stock tracking.

The servo controls only one shared dispensing movement.

The program does not use an item counting sensor, an electronic payment system or permanent inventory storage.

The LCD uses a 16-character width, so long balance values may extend beyond the visible area.

Result

The Smart Vending Machine with Stock Management demonstrates two-product selection, simulated credit input, purchase checking, servo-controlled dispensing and stock monitoring using Arduino UNO.

Product A costs ₹10 and Product B costs ₹15, with credit added in ₹5 increments.

When sufficient balance and stock are available, the program commands the servo dispensing cycle, deducts credit and reduces the selected product's stock.

Low-stock LEDs activate when the remaining stock reaches two items or fewer.

The experiment demonstrates conditional purchase logic, user input handling, inventory variables, LCD feedback and servo control.
