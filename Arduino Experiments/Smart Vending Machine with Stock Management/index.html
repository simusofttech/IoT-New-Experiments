Smart Vending Machine with Stock Management

Arduino Experiment

← All ExperimentsAimComponentsConnectionsCircuitWorkingProcedureCodeResult

Aim

To build an Arduino-based vending experiment that selects Product A or B, accepts simulated ₹5 credit, verifies stock and balance, operates a servo, and displays transaction information on an I2C LCD.

About the Experiment

Product A costs ₹10 and initially has five items. Product B costs ₹15 and initially has three items. Five buttons control selection, credit, buying and cancellation. The LCD displays status and messages; each product has a low-stock LED that turns on when its stock reaches two or fewer.

The servo demonstrates a dispensing action. Stock and credit are stored in RAM and reset when the Arduino restarts.

Controls buttons, stock, LCD and servo.

Shows product, balance, stock and messages.

Procedure

  1. Check all wiring while power is disconnected.
  2. Connect LCD VCC to 5V, GND to GND, SDA to A4 and SCL to A5.
  3. Wire the five push buttons between D6, D7, D8, D10, D11 and GND respectively.
  4. Wire both red LEDs through separate 220Ω resistors to A0 and A1; cathodes to GND.
  5. Connect the SG90 signal wire to A3, with suitable 5V power and a common ground.
  6. Install a compatible LiquidCrystal_I2C library in Arduino IDE and select Arduino UNO.
  7. Upload the program and open Serial Monitor at 9600 baud.
  8. Select a product, add ₹5 credit increments and press BUY; try Cancel and low-stock conditions.

Expected Output

  • LCD shows the selected product, stock and credit.
  • Each Credit press adds ₹5.
  • BUY with insufficient credit displays the remaining amount.
  • A successful purchase rotates the servo and decreases stock by one.
  • When stock reaches two or fewer, the matching LED lights up.
  • Cancel resets the balance to zero.

Result

The vending machine experiment demonstrates product selection, credit validation, servo-based dispensing simulation, stock tracking, low-stock indication and LCD status messages.

What You Learn

  • Interfacing a 16×2 I2C LCD with Arduino UNO.
  • Reading five buttons using INPUT_PULLUP and software debounce.
  • Controlling a servo motor using the Servo library.
  • Tracking inventory and validating a transaction.
  • Using LEDs as low-stock indicators.
