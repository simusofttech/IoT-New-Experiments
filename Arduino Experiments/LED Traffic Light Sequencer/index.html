Aim

To create a traffic light sequencing system using Arduino UNO R3, three red LEDs, three yellow LEDs and three green LEDs.

The system switches the LED groups in the order Red → Green → Yellow using predefined time intervals.

About the Experiment

The LED Traffic Light Sequence experiment demonstrates how Arduino UNO controls multiple digital outputs to simulate a traffic signal cycle.

Nine LEDs are divided into three groups: red, yellow and green.

Each group contains three LEDs, and each LED is connected to an individual Arduino digital pin.

Three red LEDs are connected to pins D2, D3 and D4.

Three yellow LEDs are connected to D5, D6 and D7.

Three green LEDs are connected to D8, D9 and D10.

Each LED includes a 220Ω current-limiting resistor in series.

When the Arduino starts, all nine LEDs are switched OFF.

The program first activates the three red LEDs together for five seconds.

After the red phase, all lights switch OFF and the three green LEDs turn ON for five seconds.

After the green phase, all lights switch OFF again and the three yellow LEDs turn ON for two seconds.

The process then repeats continuously.

Important: This experiment is a basic timed traffic light demonstration. It does not detect vehicles or pedestrians, and it is not intended to operate a real road traffic signal. There are no buttons, sensors or external communication modules in the supplied sketch.

Digital Output Initialization

During setup(), Arduino configures nine digital pins as outputs.

These pins are divided into three LED groups.

The function allLightsOff() switches all groups OFF before the sequence begins.

Red Light Phase

Arduino first switches all lights OFF, then activates RED1, RED2 and RED3 together.

The red group remains ON for 5000 milliseconds, equivalent to five seconds.

Green Light Phase

After the red phase, allLightsOff() clears the previous state.

Arduino then activates GREEN1, GREEN2 and GREEN3 for five seconds.

Yellow Light Phase

After the green phase, all LEDs are again switched OFF.

YELLOW1, YELLOW2 and YELLOW3 are switched ON for two seconds.

Once the yellow delay finishes, the loop repeats and allLightsOff() switches the LEDs OFF before starting the next red phase.

Traffic Light Timing

Red Phase 5 Seconds
Green Phase 5 Seconds
Yellow Phase 2 Seconds
Complete Cycle Approximately 12 Seconds
Complete Sequence:

Red (5 s) → Green (5 s) → Yellow (2 s) → Repeat.

The program uses blocking delay() statements. During each delay, Arduino does not evaluate additional input or control logic.

The 12-second cycle excludes the small amount of time spent executing other instructions.

Procedure

  1. Place Arduino UNO, nine LEDs, nine 220Ω resistors, the breadboard and jumper wires on the work surface.
  2. Arrange three red LEDs together on the breadboard.
  3. Arrange three yellow LEDs in a separate group.
  4. Arrange three green LEDs in a third group.
  5. Connect Red LED 1 anode to D2 through a 220Ω resistor.
  6. Connect Red LED 2 anode to D3 through a 220Ω resistor.
  7. Connect Red LED 3 anode to D4 through a 220Ω resistor.
  8. Connect Yellow LED 1 anode to D5 through a 220Ω resistor.
  9. Connect Yellow LED 2 anode to D6 through a 220Ω resistor.
  10. Connect Yellow LED 3 anode to D7 through a 220Ω resistor.
  11. Connect Green LED 1 anode to D8 through a 220Ω resistor.
  12. Connect Green LED 2 anode to D9 through a 220Ω resistor.
  13. Connect Green LED 3 anode to D10 through a 220Ω resistor.
  14. Connect all nine LED cathodes to the common GND rail.
  15. Verify LED polarity and all resistor connections.
  16. Open Arduino IDE.
  17. Create a sketch named LED_Traffic_Light_Sequencer.ino.
  18. Copy the complete Arduino code from Explore the Code.
  19. Select Arduino Uno and the correct communication port.
  20. Verify and upload the sketch.
  21. Observe the three red LEDs turning ON together.
  22. Confirm that the red LEDs remain ON for five seconds.
  23. Observe the three green LEDs turning ON next.
  24. Confirm that the green LEDs remain ON for five seconds.
  25. Observe the three yellow LEDs turning ON for two seconds.
  26. Verify that the red, green and yellow sequence repeats continuously.
  27. Try modifying the delay values to understand traffic light timing control.

delay(5000) causes a five-second pause.

delay(2000) causes a two-second pause.

These delays determine how long each LED group remains ON.

Explore more: Reusable Functions

allLightsOff() is a user-defined function that switches all nine LED pins to LOW.

Calling the function before the next phase helps ensure the previous lights are OFF.

Result

The LED Traffic Light Sequence experiment demonstrates a basic traffic signal cycle using Arduino UNO, nine LEDs and nine 220Ω resistors.

The three color groups are activated in the programmed Red → Green → Yellow sequence with fixed timing intervals.

The experiment shows how digital outputs, conditional-free sequencing, reusable functions and delays can be combined to create a timed LED system.
