Aim

To control a servo motor based on the force applied to an FSR402 Force Sensitive Resistor using Arduino UNO.

When the sensor reading exceeds the programmed threshold of 100, the servo rotates to 90°. Otherwise, it stays at 0°.

About the Experiment

The Force Sensor Controlled Servo Motor experiment demonstrates how a force-sensitive resistor can control the position of a servo motor.

The FSR402 changes its resistance according to the force applied to its sensing surface.

The FSR402 and a 10 kΩ resistor form a voltage-divider circuit.

Arduino reads the changing divider voltage through analog pin A0.

The analogRead() function converts the input into a digital value between 0 and 1023.

This value is compared with the programmed threshold of 100.

When the sensor reading becomes greater than 100, the servo moves to 90°.

When the sensor reading is 100 or lower, the servo moves back to 0°.

The sensor value and corresponding servo movement are displayed in Serial Monitor at 9600 baud.

Important: The threshold value of 100 is a raw ADC reading, not a calibrated force measurement in newtons. The experiment uses two fixed servo positions. It does not provide proportional movement between 0° and 90°.

Force Sensor Operation

The FSR402 changes resistance when force is applied to its surface.

Its resistance change affects the voltage across the 10 kΩ resistor in the voltage-divider circuit.

Analog Input Reading

Arduino measures the analog voltage through A0 using analogRead().

The reading ranges from 0 to 1023.

Threshold Comparison

The program compares the sensor value with a threshold of 100.

Program Condition:

if (forceValue > threshold)

A reading greater than 100 commands the servo to 90°.

A reading of 100 or lower commands the servo to 0°.

Servo Movement

The Servo library generates the required position-control signal on Arduino D9.

The myServo.write() function sets the programmed servo angle.

No Force / Low Reading Servo at 0°
ADC Reading Above 100 Servo at 90°
Reading Drops to 100 or Below Servo Returns to 0°
Important: The servo does not move proportionally with increasing force in this experiment. It switches between two fixed positions according to the threshold.

Procedure

  1. Place Arduino UNO, the FSR402, resistor, servo and breadboard on a stable surface.
  2. Identify the two FSR402 terminals.
  3. Connect the first FSR402 terminal to Arduino 5V.
  4. Connect the second FSR402 terminal to the A0 junction.
  5. Connect one end of the 10 kΩ resistor to the same A0 junction.
  6. Connect the other end of the resistor to Arduino GND.
  7. Connect the servo signal wire to digital pin D9.
  8. Connect servo VCC to a suitable regulated 5V supply.
  9. Connect servo GND to the common ground.
  10. Verify the circuit connections and servo power requirements.
  11. Open Arduino IDE.
  12. Ensure the Servo library is available.
  13. Create a sketch named Force_Sensor_Controlled_Servo_Motor.ino.
  14. Copy the complete Arduino program from Explore the Code.
  15. Select Arduino Uno and the correct communication port.
  16. Verify and upload the program.
  17. Open Serial Monitor at 9600 baud.
  18. Observe the initial servo position of 0°.
  19. Apply gentle pressure to the FSR402.
  20. Observe when the sensor reading exceeds the threshold of 100.
  21. Confirm the servo moves to 90°.
  22. Release the pressure.
  23. Confirm that the servo returns to 0° when the reading becomes 100 or lower.
  24. Repeat with different gentle pressure levels.

Result

The Force Sensor Controlled Servo Motor experiment demonstrates threshold-based servo control using Arduino UNO and an FSR402 Force Sensor.

Arduino measures the analog sensor signal, compares it with a threshold of 100 and commands the servo to either 0° or 90°.

The sensor reading and corresponding servo status are displayed through Serial Monitor.
