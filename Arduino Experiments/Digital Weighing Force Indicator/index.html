Aim

To develop a relative-force indicator using Arduino UNO, a Force Sensor, a 10 kΩ resistor and a 16×2 I²C LCD.

The system detects applied force, processes analog readings and displays the raw ADC value and relative force percentage.

About the Experiment

The Digital Weighing Force Indicator demonstrates how a force-sensitive resistive sensor can be interfaced with Arduino UNO.

The Force Sensor acts as a variable resistor. Its resistance changes when force is applied.

A fixed 10 kΩ resistor and the Force Sensor form a voltage-divider circuit.

Arduino measures the voltage at the common junction using analog input A0.

The program takes 20 analog readings and calculates their average to obtain a steadier value.

This average is converted from the 0–1023 ADC scale to a relative 0–100% indication.

The 16×2 I²C LCD displays both the raw averaged value and the relative force percentage.

Important: The experiment does not measure calibrated weight in grams or kilograms, or force in newtons. The percentage is calculated from the ADC reading and does not represent a calibrated, linear force measurement.

Force-Sensitive Resistance

The Force Sensor changes resistance as force is applied to its sensing surface.

This resistance change changes the voltage measured at A0.

Voltage-Divider Operation

The Force Sensor and 10 kΩ resistor form a voltage divider.

The fixed resistor connects between A0 and GND.

Sampling and Averaging

Arduino reads the analog input 20 times, with a delay of 2 milliseconds between samples.

The readings are added together and divided by 20 to calculate the average ADC value.

Relative Force Indication

The average ADC value is mapped from 0–1023 to a 0–100% scale.

The constrain() function limits the resulting percentage to the range 0–100.

Program Settings:
  • Analog Input: A0
  • ADC Range: 0–1023
  • Samples per Measurement: 20
  • Relative Display Scale: 0–100%
  • LCD Address: 0x27

Procedure

  1. Place Arduino UNO and the breadboard on a stable surface.
  2. Identify the two Force Sensor terminals.
  3. Connect one Force Sensor terminal to Arduino 5V.
  4. Connect the other Force Sensor terminal to A0.
  5. Connect one terminal of the 10 kΩ resistor to the same A0 junction.
  6. Connect the other resistor terminal to Arduino GND.
  7. Connect the 16×2 I²C LCD VCC to 5V.
  8. Connect LCD GND to GND.
  9. Connect LCD SDA to A4.
  10. Connect LCD SCL to A5.
  11. Verify the voltage-divider circuit and all LCD connections.
  12. Open Arduino IDE.
  13. Install a compatible LiquidCrystal_I2C library.
  14. Create a sketch named Digital_Weighing_Force_Indicator.ino.
  15. Copy the complete Arduino program from Explore the Code.
  16. Select Arduino Uno and the correct communication port.
  17. Verify and upload the program.
  18. Observe the initialization message on the LCD.
  19. Apply gentle pressure to the Force Sensor.
  20. Observe changes in RAW and FORCE% on the LCD.
  21. Release the pressure and observe the updated readings.
  22. Avoid exceeding the sensor's rated force or bending limits.

Result

The Digital Weighing Force Indicator experiment demonstrates a force-sensitive resistive sensor connected to Arduino UNO through a 10 kΩ voltage divider.

Arduino averages 20 readings, calculates a relative force percentage and displays both the averaged ADC value and percentage on the 16×2 I²C LCD.
