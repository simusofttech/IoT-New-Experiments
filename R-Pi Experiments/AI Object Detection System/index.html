Aim

To demonstrate a camera-based driver fatigue warning concept using Raspberry Pi 4B, OpenCV face and eye detection, an I²C LCD, an LED and a buzzer.

When eyes are not detected for approximately two seconds, the system activates the warning outputs and updates the LCD. The outputs turn off when eyes are detected again.

About the Experiment

The system captures frames from a USB webcam and uses OpenCV Haar cascade classifiers to detect a face and eyes. gpiozero controls the LED on GPIO23 and buzzer on GPIO24. A 16×2 I²C LCD at address 0x27 displays the monitoring and alert status.

A timer based on time.monotonic() starts while eyes are not detected. If that condition continues for two seconds, the program activates the LED and buzzer. When eyes are detected, it resets the timer and turns both outputs off.

Working Principle

↓

↓
Face Cascade Finds a Face
↓

↓
Eyes Detected → Reset Timer and Turn Outputs Off
↓
No Eyes for 2 Seconds → Turn LED and Buzzer On
↓
Show Status on the LCD and Camera Preview

Image Processing

Each captured frame is converted to grayscale. The face cascade searches the image; the eye cascade searches within each detected face region.

Alert Timing

When no eyes are detected, the program records a monotonic start time. After 2.0 seconds, it activates the LED and buzzer. Detecting eyes resets the timer and turns the outputs off.

Status Display

The preview draws boxes around detected faces and eyes. The LCD displays Eyes Detected, Checking Eyes or ALERT! DROWSY.

Procedure

  1. Power down the Raspberry Pi before making or changing wiring.
  2. Connect the I²C LCD to 5V, GND, GPIO2/SDA1 and GPIO3/SCL1; confirm its address is 0x27.
  3. Connect the LED to GPIO23 through the 220 Ω series resistor and connect its cathode to GND.
  4. Connect the tested low-current buzzer signal to GPIO24 and its other terminal to GND.
  5. Connect the USB webcam, power on the Pi and log in to Raspberry Pi OS.
  6. Activate the existing environment: source ~/lcd-venv/bin/activate.
  7. Change to the project directory: cd ~/driver-fatigue.
  8. Confirm both cascade XML files are present in cascades/.
  9. Run python fatigue_monitor.py.
  10. In adequate lighting, sit in front of the camera and observe the LCD and preview.
  11. For a controlled bench test only, briefly close the eyes or move out of view and observe the warning after about two seconds. Do not test while driving or operating machinery.
  12. Press Q in the camera preview to stop. Confirm the LED and buzzer turn off and the LCD shows System Stopped.

Explore More: Haar Cascades

Haar cascade classifiers use trained image features to find face and eye patterns. Detection can fail when lighting, pose, occlusion or camera quality changes.

Explore More: Monotonic Timer

time.monotonic() measures elapsed time without being affected by system clock adjustments.

Explore More: GPIO Outputs

gpiozero controls the LED on GPIO23 and buzzer on GPIO24. The program turns both off during normal eye detection and in its cleanup block.

Result

The Raspberry Pi prototype demonstrates face and eye detection from a USB webcam, LCD status updates, and LED/buzzer activation after approximately two seconds without detected eyes.

This educational prototype is not a validated drowsiness detector and must not be used to make driving-safety decisions.

  • Releasing camera, display and GPIO resources safely.
